# Composition direction: make the music breathe before making it busier

The mechanisms below guide an agent's decisions. Browser demonstrations exist for a bounded subset. They are not a claim that every control is consumed by the native compose engine.

## Four separate passes

**Identity:** choose a recognizable gesture, rhythmic cell, timbral relationship or intentional field. Record what survives variation. A memorable figure needs recurrence; maximize neither uniqueness nor randomness.

**Syntax:** decide the destination of each phrase and how an answer changes its premise. Compare 2+2+4, longer antecedents, irregular phrase spans, riff/response and deliberately static fields. A 3+2+3 phrase layout is not an odd time signature. Do not use loudness or a final tutti as the only representation of development.

**Interaction:** let roles trade foreground. Make rests in score data, not only in a gain slider. Bass should explain direction or stability; harmony may sustain while melody moves. Counterlines should respond in real gaps. Repetition can become development through reinterpretation of role, register, duration or harmonic context while preserving the nucleus.

**Performance:** apply timing placement, attacks, note lengths and dynamics after the above. Systematic placement and correlated gestures are different from independent jitter. There is no evidence that increasing random error always improves groove. The native helper in this increment only touches this final pass.

## A genre knowledge card must be more than BPM + instrument

For each proposed family, record metric groupings, strong/weak attacks, bass behavior, harmonic rhythm, voicing, ornament, phrase syntax, silence, articulation, ensemble relationship, sources and limits. Label each field as source-derived, hypothesis, prototype-implemented or listening-validated. Use practitioner/primary evidence rather than turning broad cultural descriptions into invented recipes.

The browser Atlas implements ten original, finite sketches: neo-soul/pocket, dub, UK garage, footwork, interlocking texture, minimal process, bossa, tango, ambient and broken rhythm. It crosses accompaniment grammar with melodic syntax; not all crossings will be artistically persuasive. The native engine already has some related categories. The improvement is making their causal mechanics inspectable, not claiming all ten are new genres.

Sources and specific caveats are bundled in `resources/ensemble-atelier/sources.json` (22 entries). S19 explains S01 and is not an independent study. No source recordings, phrases or commercial banks were copied. The repertoire research remains broader than the implemented demos: candombe, flamenco, son jarocho, samba, konnakol, tabla and other lines require their own idiomatic examples and practitioner review.

Do not call a temperate pentatonic interlock a traditional gamelan. Do not equate accordion with bandoneon, konnakol with generic Euclidean pulses, or every flamenco palo with a 12-count pattern. Cultural-context sources may justify further research but do not certify a generated performance.

## Bounded prototype-to-production promotion

Instrumentarium tests visual inspection and explicit edits. Pocket tests performed timing against the exact same written score. Conversation tests turn-taking and structural gaps. Atlas tests event changes caused by different grammars. Cycles tests complementary masks and their actual common return period. Keep these independent until a real user decision authorizes integration of their compositional defaults.

Promotion sequence: define the expected changed/preserved dimensions → produce a matched reduction → record a semantic diff → audition a common sound palette → test native performance/render/export → test in a scene. Save every rejected result. Level-match listening copies; do not equate higher amplitude or a nicer drawing with better composition.

## Next experiments worth building, not already implemented

1. **Articulation envelope per phrase.** Attack pressure, timbral brightness and release linked to instrument behavior. Compare isolated articulation and full phrase; provide an explicit renderer consumer. Decorative knobs do not count.
2. **Harmony with destinations.** A planner of harmonic rhythm, bass direction and non-chord-tone resolution across sections; register constraints before note emission. Test suspensions, pedal, modal borrowing and anticlimax. Do not hide bad choices by clamping pitches.
3. **Conservation-aware local rewriting.** Stable event IDs and region hashes, frozen A/return, editable B, conflicts before applying. A browser-specific protected phrase is not a universal native recompose command.
4. **Timbre parity and acoustic QA.** Resolve the exact sample/patch in each backend, content-addressed render cache, channel-safe MIDI, release-aware voice budget and measured loop seams. These production defects remain more urgent than enlarging the catalog.
5. **Game-state musical transitions.** A quiet cue, tension, conflict and aftermath sharing identity, with harmonic entry/exit checks, hysteresis and stingers. A bar-quantized button alone is not a complete music director.

For expression design, see Roger Linn's primary instrument documentation; for event/display clocks, the W3C Web Audio specification. For the distinction between beneficial systematic microtiming and arbitrary errors, use the original studies in S01/S02. None validates our specific presets or replaces listening.
